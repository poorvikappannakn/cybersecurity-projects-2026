# Risk Evaluation Metric

## Purpose

The risk evaluation metric is used to consistently assess and prioritize cybersecurity risks identified within an SME.

The metric considers two primary factors:

- Likelihood — the probability that a threat could successfully occur.
- Impact — the potential effect of the threat on the organization's operations, information, finances, and reputation.

## Risk Score

Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact

Both Likelihood and Impact are evaluated on a scale from 1 to 5.

### Likelihood Scale

| Score | Rating | Description |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Very Low | The threat is highly unlikely to occur. |
| 2 | Low | The threat is unlikely but possible. |
| 3 | Moderate | The threat has a realistic possibility of occurring. |
| 4 | High | The threat is likely to occur. |
| 5 | Very High | The threat is highly likely to occur. |

### Impact Scale

| Score | Rating | Description |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Very Low | Minimal effect on the organization. |
| 2 | Low | Limited operational or financial effect. |
| 3 | Moderate | Noticeable disruption or loss. |
| 4 | High | Significant operational, financial, or information-security impact. |
| 5 | Very High | Severe impact that could critically affect business operations or sensitive information. |

## Risk Classification

| Risk Score | Risk Level | Priority |
|---:|---|---|
| 1–4 | Low | Monitor |
| 5–9 | Moderate | Planned Action |
| 10–16 | High | Prioritized Action |
| 17–25 | Critical | Immediate Attention |

## Risk Assessment Process

Threat
→ Likelihood Assessment
→ Impact Assessment
→ Risk Score
→ Risk Classification
→ Risk Priority

## Residual Risk

After recommended security controls are implemented, the risk is reassessed using the same metric.

This allows the framework to compare the estimated risk before and after mitigation and determine whether additional controls are required.
