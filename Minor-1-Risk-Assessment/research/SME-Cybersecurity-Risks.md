# Cybersecurity Risks in Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs)

## 1. Introduction

Small and medium enterprises (SMEs) face significant cybersecurity risks despite often having fewer security resources than larger organizations. Cyberattacks can affect business operations, customer information, financial resources, and organizational reputation.

The purpose of this research is to identify common cybersecurity risks affecting SMEs and understand their potential impact. The findings will be used in the later stages of this project to develop a cybersecurity risk assessment framework.

## 2. Common Cybersecurity Risks

### 2.1 Phishing

Phishing is a social engineering technique in which attackers use deceptive emails or other messages to trick users into opening malicious links, downloading harmful software, or revealing sensitive information.

Phishing is particularly relevant to SMEs because employees are often targeted as an entry point into an organization's systems.

According to the UK Government's Cyber Security Breaches Survey 2025, phishing was the most prevalent type of breach or attack among organizations that experienced a cyber incident. It was reported by 85% of businesses that had experienced a breach or attack in the previous 12 months.

**Potential impact on SMEs:**
- Credential theft
- Unauthorized account access
- Malware infection
- Financial loss
- Data exposure

**Sources:** NIST; UK Government Cyber Security Breaches Survey 2025

---

### 2.2 Ransomware

Ransomware is a type of malicious software that prevents users from accessing systems or data, commonly by encrypting files, and may demand payment from the victim.

Ransomware can have a particularly serious effect on SMEs because disruption to critical systems and data can directly affect day-to-day business operations.

A 2025 analysis of SMB breaches reported ransomware as a major threat to small and medium businesses, accounting for a large proportion of reported SMB breaches.

**Potential impact on SMEs:**
- Loss of access to business data
- Operational disruption
- Recovery costs
- Possible data exposure
- Financial losses

**Sources:** NIST; 2025 SMB threat analysis

---

### 2.3 Insider Threats

An insider threat occurs when an individual with authorized access to an organization's systems, resources, or network misuses that access. Insider threats may be intentional or accidental.

Employees, contractors, vendors, and other trusted individuals can potentially become sources of security incidents.

**Potential impact on SMEs:**
- Unauthorized access to sensitive information
- Data theft or disclosure
- Fraud
- Accidental data exposure
- Disruption of business operations

Insider threats are particularly important for SMEs because employees may have access to multiple systems or resources due to smaller organizational structures.

**Source:** Small-business insider threat guidance

---

### 2.4 Weak Authentication and Credential Theft

Weak authentication includes the use of weak or reused passwords, insufficient authentication controls, and the absence of multi-factor authentication (MFA).

Compromised credentials can allow attackers to gain unauthorized access to business accounts and systems.

A 2025 SMB threat analysis reported that stolen credentials were involved in a significant proportion of SMB breaches.

**Potential impact on SMEs:**
- Account takeover
- Unauthorized access
- Data theft
- Further compromise of business systems
- Increased risk from password reuse

**Possible security measures:**
- Strong password policies
- Multi-factor authentication
- Password managers
- Phishing-resistant authentication where appropriate
- Employee security awareness

**Sources:** NIST; 2025 SMB threat analysis

---

### 2.5 Unpatched and Vulnerable Software

Software that is outdated or contains known vulnerabilities can provide attackers with opportunities to gain unauthorized access or execute malicious actions.

SMEs may face difficulties maintaining consistent patch management because of limited technical resources, staff, or formal security processes.

**Potential impact on SMEs:**
- Unauthorized system access
- Malware infection
- Data compromise
- Service disruption
- Exploitation of known vulnerabilities

Regular software updates, vulnerability management, and maintaining an inventory of systems and applications can help reduce this risk.

**Source:** 2025 SMB cybersecurity guidance

---

### 2.6 Data Breaches

A data breach occurs when confidential or sensitive information is accessed, disclosed, or stolen by unauthorized individuals.

SMEs may store valuable information such as customer details, employee information, financial records, business documents, and credentials.

**Potential impact on SMEs:**
- Loss of confidential information
- Financial losses
- Loss of customer trust
- Legal or regulatory consequences
- Business disruption

Data breaches can result from phishing, compromised credentials, vulnerable systems, insider threats, or other security weaknesses.

**Source:** NIST Cybersecurity Glossary

---

## 3. Research Findings

The research indicates that SMEs face a combination of human, technical, and organizational cybersecurity risks.

The most important risks identified for this project are:

| Risk | Main Security Concern |
|---|---|
| Phishing | Social engineering and credential theft |
| Ransomware | Loss of access to systems and data |
| Insider Threat | Misuse of authorized access |
| Weak Authentication | Account compromise |
| Unpatched Software | Exploitation of known vulnerabilities |
| Data Breach | Unauthorized access or disclosure of information |

These risks will be used as the foundation for the risk assessment model developed in the next stage of this project.

## 4. Conclusion

The research shows that cybersecurity risks affecting SMEs are not limited to a single type of attack. Human factors such as phishing and insider threats can combine with technical weaknesses such as weak authentication and unpatched software.

Understanding these risks is the first step toward developing an effective cybersecurity risk assessment framework. The next stage of this project will use the identified risks to develop a structured risk assessment model and evaluation metric.
